# Collection expansion — 27 November 2026

The expansion proceeds in two editorial passes: build a broad, deduplicated roster with documented life dates and category assignments; then add original German/English profiles and dated works or contributions supported by the consulted sources.

Each research note records the sources and uncertainties behind its category batch. The JSON collections contain publishable entries; further candidates in research notes are a backlog, not silently included in the atlas. Category ownership is only a division of editorial work. A person has one permanent record and may belong to several categories.

The scientists category automatically includes its subject subcategories. Women in science and research is an additional cross-disciplinary selection, explicitly including mathematics. Dedicated audits check these overlaps after the category batches are assembled.

Coverage aims to expand the range of eras, regions, traditions and genders. This is an expanding curated collection, not an exhaustive register or a ranking of human importance. Availability of sources still shapes coverage. Political influence, scientific contribution and artistic achievement are described in their historical contexts, including documented harms where relevant.

Sources are consulted for the specific records and claims, with uncertainty kept in dates and notes. Structural checks and source consultation do not replace independent historical or native-language review; both remain pending. Portrait expansion is separate and requires documented reuse rights.

## Integration decisions for the initial expansion

The final merge contains 381 unique people. The category-fix manifests record applied additions for Karl Marx and Ibn Khaldun (social research), Rosa Luxemburg (economic research), and Margaret Thatcher (documented industrial chemistry work before politics). Luxemburg and Thatcher retain their political categories; the supporting sources and research context are included in their public records. These memberships describe documented work, not endorsement of political positions. Marie Curie also belongs to natural scientists because that category includes chemistry; her existing record documents both fields.

The women-in-science audit now covers 77 records, including all 27 women assigned to mathematics. People may appear in multiple category rosters, but are stored and displayed once when selections are combined. See [the final coverage index](../docs/COVERAGE.md) for the edition's counts and roster.
