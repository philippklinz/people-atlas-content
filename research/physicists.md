# Physics and astronomy expansion research

Checked against web source text on 2026-11-27. This is an introductory expansion, not a complete or representative history. The category combines physics and astronomy. The modern category labels are navigation aids; they are not claims about every historical person’s own disciplinary identity.

## Scope and editorial state

- 30 new records in `people/expansion-physicists.json`, with German and English summaries, biographies and 1–3 dated contributions.
- All 30 people are deceased. Seven women retain `physicists` and additionally belong to `women-in-science`; Somerville also belongs to `mathematicians`.
- No portraits added. No existing IDs or records changed. Ibn al-Haytham was excluded because the collection already contains him.
- Source-backed draft; independent historical review and native-language review remain pending. The selection still has substantial geographic, chronological and disciplinary gaps.

## Uncertainty and source decisions

- Al-Biruni: MacTutor gives 1048, while the Iranica life entry argues for a death after 1050. The record uses `death.precision = unknown` and a bilingual date note. No invented upper bound was supplied; the current engine should keep this person in the list without an invented lifespan bar.
- Halley: the displayed birth date is approximate, following the qualification documented in the linked biography.
- Hawking: the 1974 radiation date follows Cambridge. MacTutor’s earlier date was not adopted. The contribution is described as a theoretical prediction.
- Salam: Imperial’s indexed page text supported the biography and 1968 publication date. Direct extraction returned HTTP 403; ICTP’s own history page was directly readable and supported the 1964 foundation.
- Cambridge’s Hawking page was available through indexed text; direct opening returned HTTP 403. That access distinction is recorded here rather than presenting the blocked response as readable.
- Rubin: the wording avoids an exclusive discovery claim. The accompanying PNAS remembrance supplies context for earlier dark-matter evidence.
- Meitner: unnecessary doctorate-year details were omitted because the consulted institutional summaries did not resolve the discrepancy with other commonly reported dates.
- Huygens: the clock-patent year was omitted rather than carrying forward an unclear date. Fermi’s statistics-publication year was likewise not used.
- Academic and institutional summaries were paraphrased in original prose. Each single-source record contains fewer than 200 prose words across both languages, including its date note; short evidence-index entries also stay within the applicable source budget. Al-Biruni uses two sources for the longer record and its date disagreement.
