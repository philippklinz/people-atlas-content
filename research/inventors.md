# Inventor expansion research

Consulted: 2026-11-27. Collection: `people/expansion-inventors.json`.

## Scope and review state

This batch contains 30 new people. It is a broad initial roster, not an exhaustive or ranked history of invention. Each record has original German and English summaries, short biographies and one to three dated contributions. Existing Gutenberg, Tesla, Lovelace, Leonardo and Lamarr records were left intact. The collection uses the existing schema and categories; no portraits or unsupported image licences were added.

Institutional museum, archive, government, university and inventor-hall material supplied the evidence. Corporate histories are used for their own institutional chronology and specific engineering records; promotional language and unsupported priority rankings were not reproduced. The Su Song entry also uses historical research published by the Max Planck Institute of Geoanthropology. Source material was consulted through retrieved pages or substantive indexed extracts. The Smithsonian Zhang Heng page and Columbia’s Su Song page timed out on direct open; Smithsonian’s indexed record was available, while Columbia is not a record source. The Max Planck working paper opened successfully; subsequent find requests failed, so only the retrieved introductory evidence on the Su Song chapter is used.

All 30 records passed the person JSON schema. Duplicate IDs, referenced category IDs, birth/death ordering, highlight dates, bilingual fields, biography lengths and source fields were checked separately. The repository checker currently covers only the 28 people in the pre-integration catalog and passed for that catalog. Adding the new collection to the catalog, recalculating the translation fingerprint and validating the integrated dataset belongs to the parent task.

Independent historical review and independent German/English language review remain pending. These records must not be marked as independently reviewed merely because structural validation passed.

## Category decisions

All records belong to `inventors`. Cochrane, Knight, Anderson and Kwolek also belong to `women-in-science`, retaining their subject memberships. Kwolek and Carver are also `naturalists`; Marconi, Su Song and Zhang Heng are also `physicists` (which includes astronomy); Zhang Heng is also `mathematicians`; Morse is also `artists`. Parent `scientists` selection follows the existing category hierarchy. There is one record per person, including the two Wright brothers as separate people with shared-work context.

## Next research priorities

The roster still overrepresents European and North American industrial invention and men. A further pass should investigate documented contributions from African, South Asian, Southeast Asian and Indigenous contexts, including collaborative knowledge traditions that cannot responsibly be assigned to a single named inventor. More technical researchers who were women should be included alongside the existing subject categories.

Candidate backlog requiring fresh source research and duplicate reconciliation: Janaki Ammal, Yvonne Brill, Patricia Bath, Marion Donovan, Beulah Louise Henry, Ann Tsukamoto, Valérie Thomas, Tabitha Babbit (especially the contested attribution), Elijah McCoy, Lonnie Johnson, Fazlur Rahman Khan, Sake Dean Mahomed and Jagadish Chandra Bose. These are research leads, not verified additions or claims of invention.

For a deeper biography pass, consult specialist work on patent disputes and collaborators, industrial labour, colonial and military uses, and barriers to education and credit. Source-based context on Morse’s politics, Bell’s oralism and eugenics, and Marconi’s fascist affiliations is specifically still needed before writing fuller biographies. The current concise entries do not claim to provide complete assessments of these lives.
