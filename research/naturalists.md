# Natural sciences expansion — research record

Collection: `people/expansion-naturalists.json`. Research access date: **2026-11-27**.

This initial expansion contains **31 new people**, **43 highlights** and original English/German summaries and short biographies. All 31 retain `naturalists`; eight women additionally have `women-in-science`. The biographies are 42–51 words per language. This is a researched starting collection, not a complete history of world science.

## Evidence and review status

Each record links to institutional biographies, archival catalogue descriptions, scholarly reference entries or indexed scholarly articles that support its dates and principal contributions. Some pages were available as search-index excerpts when direct retrieval returned a restriction or timeout (notably Library of Congress entries and the Royal Society Leeuwenhoek letter). These sources were consulted through the retrieved evidence; this was not a complete live-link availability audit.

All prose was newly written; no portraits or source-text extracts were added. Dates and claims were checked against the listed evidence, but **independent historical review and native-language review remain pending**. Fuller biographies should add further sources before expanding claims. The evidence column below is an index to the record’s source set, not a separate historical certification.

## Chronology and attribution decisions

- **Hippocrates:** traditional approximate dates are explicitly qualified. The corpus contains multiple authors; no surviving text is assigned securely to him. Its highlight therefore has an unknown date.
- **Galen:** the display uses approximately 200 for death and explains the alternative tradition of 216. The writing highlight is approximate rather than an invented exact publication date.
- **Ibn Sina:** the conventional approximate birth date of 980 is retained with a note that the Stanford reference argues for roughly 970 or earlier. The Canon highlight has an unknown date rather than a convenient but unverified completion year.
- **Ibn al-Nafis:** approximate 1213 follows the historical article; the Library of Congress also reports 1210/1211. Both readings are disclosed in `dateNote`; the commentary date is approximate.
- **Paracelsus:** birth remains approximate. **Mendel:** the 1865 presentations are distinguished from the paper’s 1866 publication.
- Contributions produced collaboratively are described accordingly, including Merian/Dorothea, Lavoisier/Marie-Anne, Humboldt/Bonpland, Wallace/Ali, Franklin/Gosling and Fleming/Florey/Chain. Unsupported “first ever” claims are avoided.

## Coverage still to develop

This collection spans Greek and Islamic medicine, European natural history and laboratory sciences, and Indian botanical and chemical research. Coverage remains weighted toward European institutions and needs substantial expansion in East Asia, Africa, Latin America, Indigenous knowledge traditions and further women’s scientific work.

Unresearched candidates for a later pass include al-Razi, al-Zahrawi, Ibn al-Baytar, Shen Kuo, Li Shizhen, Jagadish Chandra Bose, Salim Ali, Fe del Mundo, Agnes Arber, Roger Arliner Young, Ernest Everett Just and Marie Tharp. These names are a research backlog, not validated records or a claim to exhaustiveness.

The next biography pass can add specific publications, research settings, collaborators, barriers to institutional recognition and contested legacies with additional evidence. Asima Chatterjee currently has an award highlight tied to documented research; dated individual papers would improve this record.
